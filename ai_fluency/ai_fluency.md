## 1. Introduction: What Is AI Fluency?

**AI Fluency** is the ability to work with AI systems in ways that are **effective, efficient, ethical, and safe**. It includes practical skills, knowledge, insights, and values that help you adapt as AI technologies evolve.

The **AI Fluency Framework** organizes this ability into **four interconnected competencies**, known as the **4Ds**:

| Competency | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| **Delegation** | Setting goals and deciding whether, when, and how to engage with AI |
| **Description** | Effectively describing goals to prompt useful AI behaviors and outputs |
| **Discernment** | Accurately assessing the usefulness of AI outputs and behaviors |
| **Diligence** | Taking responsibility for what we do with AI and how we do it |

Each competency is an interconnected collection of **skills, knowledge, insights, and values**. They are not a strict sequence: they feed into each other continuously (for example, Discernment and Description form a feedback loop).

## 2. Foundations: Understanding Generative AI

### 2.1 What is Generative AI?

Generative AI refers to AI systems that can **create new content** rather than just analyzing existing data.

| Traditional AI | Generative AI |
|---|---|
| Classifies emails as spam or not spam | Can write a completely new email for you |

### 2.2 Three Pillars That Made It Possible

1. **Algorithms**: The Transformer architecture (2017) revolutionized the processing of long text passages.
2. **Data explosion**: A huge growth in digital data (websites, code repositories, and other text) provided the raw material for training.
3. **Computing power**: Massive increases in computational power (chips like GPUs) made it possible to train models on all that data.

### 2.3 How It Works

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Pre-training** | Models analyze billions of text examples, learning to predict what comes next. |
| **Fine-tuning** | Models are refined to follow instructions, be helpful, and avoid harmful content. |
| **Deployment** | Users provide prompts, and the model generates responses based on the prompts and patterns learned during training. |

### 2.4 Key Capabilities

- Versatile language skills
- General-purpose abilities
- Learning from examples
- Connecting to external tools and data

### 2.5 Current Limitations

- **Knowledge cutoff date**: no built-in knowledge of events after training
- **Potential inaccuracies ("hallucinations")**: confident but incorrect statements
- **Context window constraints**: only so much information can be considered at once
- **Challenges with complex reasoning and math**

> **Why this matters:** Knowing both the capabilities and the limitations is the basis for good Delegation (what to hand to AI) and good Discernment (what to double-check).

## 3. The Three Modes of AI Interaction

| Mode | Description | Who is in control? |
|---|---|---|
| **Automation** | AI executes specific tasks based on specific human instructions. The human defines what needs to be done; the AI carries it out. | Human defines the task |
| **Augmentation** | Humans and AI collaborate as thinking partners, with iterative back-and-forth where both contribute. | Shared |
| **Agency** | Humans configure AI to work independently on their behalf, including interacting with other humans or AI. The human sets up the AI's knowledge and behavior patterns rather than specifying exact actions. | AI acts independently within human-set boundaries |

## 4. The 4D Framework at a Glance

| Competency | Sub-competencies |
|---|---|
| **Delegation** | Problem Awareness, Platform Awareness, Task Delegation |
| **Description** | Product Description, Process Description, Performance Description |
| **Discernment** | Product Discernment, Process Discernment, Performance Discernment |
| **Diligence** | Creation Diligence, Transparency Diligence, Deployment Diligence |

**Pattern to notice:** Description and Discernment share the same three-part structure: **Product** (the output), **Process** (how the AI gets there), and **Performance** (how the AI behaves during the collaboration). What you describe is what you later evaluate.

## 5. Delegation

**Definition:** Making thoughtful decisions about what work is appropriate for you to do, for AI to do, or for you and AI to do together, and how to distribute those tasks.

### 5.1 Three Sub-competencies

#### Problem Awareness
- **What it is:** Clearly understanding your goals and the nature of the work *before* involving AI.
- **Guiding question:** *"What am I trying to do?"*

#### Platform Awareness
- **What it is:** Understanding the capabilities and limitations of different AI systems.
- **Guiding question:** *"What do you do well?"*

#### Task Delegation
- **What it is:** Thoughtfully distributing work between humans and AI to leverage the strengths of each.
- **Guiding statement:** *"Ok, here's the plan."*

### 5.2 Key Takeaway

> Effective delegation requires **both domain expertise and an understanding of AI capabilities**.

---

## 6. Description

**Definition:** The ability to communicate with AI in ways that create a productive, collaborative environment.

### 6.1 Three Sub-competencies

#### Product Description
- **What it is:** Defining what you want in terms of **outputs, format, audience, and style**.
- **Illustrative contrast:** "Hey! Guess what I'm thinking?" (vague) versus a clearly specified request.

#### Process Description
- **What it is:** Defining **how the AI approaches your request**, such as providing step-by-step instructions for the AI to follow.
- **Illustrative phrase:** "The first thing we need to do..."

#### Performance Description
- **What it is:** Defining the AI's **behavior during your collaboration**, such as whether it should be concise or detailed, challenging or supportive.
- **Illustrative phrase:** "Act as a Socratic tutor."

### 6.2 Key Takeaway

> Clear communication with AI systems up front **saves time and leads to better results**.

---

## 7. Discernment

**Definition:** The ability to thoughtfully and critically evaluate what AI produces, how it produces it, and how it behaves.

### 7.1 Three Sub-competencies

#### Product Discernment
- **What it is:** Evaluating the **quality of what AI produces**.
- **Criteria:** accuracy, appropriateness, coherence, relevance.

#### Process Discernment
- **What it is:** Evaluating **how the AI arrived at its output**.
- **Look for:** logical errors, lapses in attention, or inappropriate reasoning steps.

#### Performance Discernment
- **What it is:** Evaluating **how the AI behaves during your interaction**.
- **Consider:** whether its communication style is effective for your needs.

### 7.2 Key Takeaway

> Discernment works hand-in-hand with Description in a **continuous feedback loop**: you describe, evaluate the result, then refine your description.

```
   Description  ──►  AI output/behavior  ──►  Discernment
        ▲                                          │
        └──────────── refine your request ◄────────┘
```

---

## 8. Diligence

**Definition:** Taking responsibility for what we do with AI and how we do it.

### 8.1 Three Sub-competencies

#### Creation Diligence
- **What it is:** Being thoughtful about **which AI systems you use and how you interact with them**.

#### Transparency Diligence
- **What it is:** Being **honest about AI's role in your work** with everyone who needs to know.
- **Example from the course:** Stating "In this document, Claude 3.7 ..." to disclose AI involvement.

#### Deployment Diligence
- **What it is:** Taking responsibility for **verifying and vouching for the outputs** you use or share.

### 8.2 Key Takeaway

> Different contexts (**personal, academic, professional**) may have different expectations for disclosure and verification.

## 9. Six Techniques for Effective Prompting

These techniques are practical applications of **Description**. Each includes a before/after illustration.

### Technique 1: Provide Context

Be specific about scope, geography, timeframe, and other relevant parameters.

| | Prompt |
|---|---|
| **Before** | *Tell me about climate change.* |
| **After** | *Explain three major impacts of climate change on agriculture in tropical regions, with examples from the past decade.* |

### Technique 2: Show Examples of What "Good" Looks Like

Examples help the AI understand the pattern, style, or format you want more clearly than descriptions alone.

| | Prompt |
|---|---|
| **Before** | *Please convert this technical statement to plain language: "The platform implements end-to-end encryption protocols to safeguard data integrity."* |
| **After** | *Here are two examples of how to convert technical jargon into plain language:* <br>*1. "The quantum algorithm exhibits quadratic speedup." becomes "The new method solves problems roughly twice as fast as previous methods."* <br>*2. "The interface leverages intuitive design paradigms." becomes "The design is easy to understand and use."* <br>*Now, please convert this technical statement to plain language: "The platform implements end-to-end encryption protocols to safeguard data integrity."* |

### Technique 3: Specify Output Constraints

State the format, structure, sections, style, and features you want.

| | Prompt |
|---|---|
| **Before** | *Design me a personal art portfolio website.* |
| **After** | *Create a clean, modern single-page portfolio website with these sections: Hero, About Me, Skills, Portfolio/Projects, Experience, and Contact. Make the navigation menu sticky and responsive, with a hamburger menu on mobile. Use a sunset color palette and add a dark/light mode toggle.* |

### Technique 4: Break Complex Tasks into Steps

Clear steps guide the AI's reasoning process and ensure thorough, methodical responses.

| | Prompt |
|---|---|
| **Before** | *Analyze this quarterly sales data.* |
| **After** | *I'd like to analyze this quarterly sales data. Please approach this by:* <br>*1. Identifying top-performing products* <br>*2. Comparing current quarter to previous quarter* <br>*3. Highlighting unusual patterns or trends* <br>*4. Suggesting possible reasons for these trends* |

### Technique 5: Ask It to Think First

Giving the AI space to think before responding encourages more thoughtful, comprehensive answers.

> *Before answering, please think through this problem carefully. Consider the different factors involved, potential constraints, and various approaches before recommending the best solution.*

### Technique 6: Define the AI's Role

Defining the AI's role, tone, or style helps shape its approach to fit your needs and audience.

> *Please explain how rainbows form from the perspective of an experienced science teacher speaking to a bright 10-year-old who's interested in science.*

### Secret Weapon: Ask the AI for Help with Prompting

When you are not sure how to ask for something, the AI can help improve your prompt. The course calls this *perhaps the most powerful technique of all*.

> *I'm trying to get you, Claude, to help me with [goal]. I'm not sure how to phrase my request to get the best results. Can you help me craft an effective prompt for this?*

### 9.1 Techniques Mapped to the 4Ds

| Technique | Main Description type |
|---|---|
| 1. Provide context | Product Description |
| 2. Show examples | Product Description |
| 3. Specify output constraints | Product Description |
| 4. Break tasks into steps | Process Description |
| 5. Ask it to think first | Process Description |
| 6. Define the AI's role | Performance Description |

## 10. Key Terminology Glossary

### 10.1 Core AI Fluency Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **AI Fluency** | The ability to work with AI systems in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical, and safe. |
| **The 4Ds** | The four core competencies: Delegation, Description, Discernment, and Diligence. |
| **Delegation** | Deciding what work is done by humans, what by AI, and how to distribute tasks between them. |
| **Description** | Effectively communicating with AI: defining outputs, guiding processes, and specifying behaviors. |
| **Discernment** | Thoughtfully and critically evaluating AI outputs, processes, behaviors, and interactions. |
| **Diligence** | Using AI responsibly and ethically, including transparency and accountability. |

### 10.2 Human-AI Interaction Modes

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Automation** | AI performs specific tasks based on specific human instructions. |
| **Augmentation** | Humans and AI collaborate as thinking partners through iterative back-and-forth. |
| **Agency** | Humans configure AI to work independently on their behalf, including interacting with other humans or AI. |

### 10.3 AI Technical Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Generative AI** | AI systems that create new content (text, images, code, etc.) rather than just analyzing existing data. |
| **Large language models (LLMs)** | Generative AI systems trained on vast amounts of text to understand and generate human language. |
| **Claude** | Anthropic's family of large language models. |
| **Parameters** | Mathematical values within a model that determine how it processes information and relates pieces of language. Modern LLMs contain billions. |
| **Neural networks** | Computing systems similar to, but distinct from, biological brains; interconnected nodes in layers that learn patterns from data. |
| **Transformer architecture** | The 2017 breakthrough design enabling LLMs to process text in parallel while attending to relationships between words across long passages. |
| **Scaling laws** | Empirical observation that larger models trained on more data with more compute improve in consistent patterns; new capabilities can emerge at certain scale thresholds. |
| **Pre-training** | The initial phase where models learn patterns from vast text, building foundational language and knowledge understanding. |
| **Fine-tuning** | Additional training so models follow instructions, give helpful responses, and avoid harmful content. |
| **Context window** | The amount of information an AI can consider at one time, including conversation history and shared documents. Has a model-specific maximum. |
| **Hallucination** | An error where the AI confidently states something plausible but incorrect. |
| **Knowledge cutoff date** | The point after which a model has no built-in knowledge of the world. |
| **Reasoning / thinking models** | Models designed to think step by step through complex problems, improving performance on logical reasoning tasks. |
| **Temperature** | A setting controlling response randomness: higher gives more varied, creative output (like boiling water); lower gives more predictable, focused output (like ice crystals). |
| **Retrieval augmented generation (RAG)** | A technique connecting models to external knowledge sources to improve accuracy and reduce hallucinations. |
| **Bias** | Systematic patterns in AI outputs that unfairly favor or disadvantage certain groups or perspectives, often reflecting training data. |

### 10.4 Prompt Engineering Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Prompt** | The input given to an AI model, including instructions and any shared documents. |
| **Prompt engineering** | Designing effective prompts to produce desired outputs; combines clear communication with AI-specific techniques. |
| **Chain-of-thought prompting** | Encouraging the AI to work step by step, breaking complex tasks into smaller steps. |
| **Few-shot learning (n-shot prompting)** | Teaching by showing examples of the desired input-output pattern; "N" is the number of examples. |
| **Role or persona definition** | Specifying a character, expertise level, or communication style for the AI (e.g., "speak as a UX design expert"). |
| **Output constraints / formatting** | Specifying the desired format, length, structure, or other characteristics of the response. |
| **Think-first approach** | Asking the AI to work through its reasoning before giving a final answer. |

## 11. Quick Reference and Review Questions

### 11.1 One-Page Summary

- **AI Fluency** = effective, efficient, ethical, and safe work with AI.
- **Delegation**: *Should I use AI for this, and how?* (Problem, Platform, Task)
- **Description**: *How do I tell the AI what I need?* (Product, Process, Performance)
- **Discernment**: *Is what I got any good?* (Product, Process, Performance)
- **Diligence**: *Am I being responsible?* (Creation, Transparency, Deployment)
- **Modes**: Automation, Augmentation, Agency.
- **Limitations to remember**: knowledge cutoff, hallucinations, context window limits, complex reasoning and math.

### 11.2 Review Questions

1. How does Generative AI differ from traditional AI? Give an example of each.
2. What are the three pillars that made generative AI possible?
3. Describe the three stages of how generative AI works (pre-training, fine-tuning, deployment).
4. Distinguish Automation, Augmentation, and Agency with a short example of each.
5. What are the three sub-competencies of Delegation, and what question does each answer?
6. How do Product, Process, and Performance Description differ?
7. Why do the course materials describe Description and Discernment as a "continuous feedback loop"?
8. What is the difference between Creation, Transparency, and Deployment Diligence?
9. Rewrite the vague prompt "Tell me about climate change" using at least two of the six prompting techniques.
10. Why might disclosure and verification expectations differ between personal, academic, and professional contexts?

### 11.3 Practice Activity

Pick a real task you do regularly (e.g., summarizing a report, planning a project, drafting an email). Walk through the 4Ds:

1. **Delegation**: What is the goal? Which parts suit AI, and which should stay with you?
2. **Description**: Write a prompt that specifies product, process, and performance.
3. **Discernment**: Evaluate the output for accuracy, reasoning, and communication style. What would you change?
4. **Diligence**: What needs verifying before you share it? Who needs to know AI was involved?
